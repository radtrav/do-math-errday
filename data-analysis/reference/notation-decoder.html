The Notation Decoder

Math notation is a terse programming language with a spoken form. Every expression below comes three ways: the symbols, how you'd say it out loud, and the TypeScript it compiles to.

The pronunciation atoms

These few rules let you read any expression you meet in the wild, not just the ones below. Two grouping tricks: parentheses resolve inside-out, and the spoken parenthesis is the phrase …all over… / a pause.

Sums and products

NotationSay it out loudTypeScriptNotes
Σi=1n xi the sum, over i from one to n, of x-sub-icasually: "add up all the x's" xs.reduce((a, x) => a + x, 0) Σ is a for-loop with an accumulator. The bounds are the loop range.
Πi xi the product, over i, of x-sub-icasually: "multiply all the x's together" xs.reduce((a, x) => a * x, 1) Same loop, × instead of +. Lifts compound this way.
xi x sub icasually: "the i-th x" xs[i] Subscript = array index. Superscript = a power (x², x³) — context disambiguates.

Averages and expectation

NotationSay it out loudTypeScriptNotes
x̄ = (1/n) Σ xi x-bar equals one over n, times the sum of the x-icasually: "x-bar is the average of the x's" sum(xs) / xs.length A bar over a variable = "average of the data I actually have."
E[X] the expected value of Xcasually: "E of X" mean(draw(Infinity)) The mean over infinite draws — a property of the process, not the sample. x̄ estimates E[X].
μ, σ mew, sigma"the true mean", "the true spread" // true mean, true sd Greek = the process's true value (unknowable); Latin (x̄, s) = your estimate from data.

Spread

NotationSay it out loudTypeScriptNotes
Var(X) = E[(X − μ)²] the variance of X equals the expected value of, X minus mew, squaredcasually: "the average squared distance from the mean" mean(xs.map(x => (x - m) ** 2)) Squaring kills signs and punishes outliers.
σ = √Var(X) sigma equals the square root of the variance of Xcasually: "root Var" Math.sqrt(variance) Back in the original units. "Daily AOV lift swings ±85%" is a σ.
Cov(X, Y) the covariance of X and Ycasually: "Cov of X and Y" mean(zip(xs,ys).map(([x,y]) => (x-mx)*(y-my))) Positive = they move together. The 2·Cov term is why co-moving legs amplify RPV swings.
ρ, r row / r"the correlation of X and Y" cov(xs, ys) / (sd(xs) * sd(ys)) Covariance rescaled to −1…+1. ρ = true, r = estimated from your sample.

p̂ p-hat"the observed rate" — an estimate of p const pHat = O / V; The hat means "computed from data, not the truth." p̂ estimates p the way x̄ estimates μ.
SE(p̂) = √(p(1−p)/n) the standard error of p-hat equals the square root of: p, times one-minus-p, all over nnote the spoken parenthesis: "all over n" groups the whole numerator Math.sqrt(p * (1 - p) / n) The σ of the estimate itself: how much p̂ wobbles across identical days. Growth, ratios, logs

NotationSay it out loudTypeScriptNotes
ℓ = T/C − 1 ell equals T over C, minus onecasually: "the lift" test / control - 1 Our convention (ℓ, script L) for arm-vs-arm relative difference.
log(ab) = log a + log b log of a-b equals log a plus log bcasually: "the log of a product is the sum of the logs" Math.log(a * b) === Math.log(a) + Math.log(b) The most useful identity in this course: multiplicative worlds (RPV = CVR·AOV) become additive, so variance decomposes.
ex, exp(x) e to the xthe inverse of log: it converts log points back to a ratio Math.exp(x) // Math.exp(Math.log(r)) === r Round trip: ratio → log points (add/decompose there) → exp back to a ratio. e0.0304 = 1.031 turned the mobile month's log sum back into +3.1%.
y ∝ 1/√n y is proportional to one over root n"same shape, unknown constant" y = k / Math.sqrt(n) // some constant k Sampling error ∝ 1/√n is lesson 0002's core fact.
≈, ≡ is about / is identically equal to≡ also reads "is true by definition" closeTo(a, b) / true by construction ≡ means algebra guarantees it (RPV ≡ CVR·AOV); ≈ means estimation is involved.
Reading rule Read fractions top first, resolve parentheses inside-out, and let all over and pauses do the grouping. If an expression still feels opaque after saying it aloud, expand it into the loop or filter it stands for — the TS column — and it will always be something you already knew.
