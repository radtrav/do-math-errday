Glossary

The course's shared vocabulary. Every lesson uses these terms exactly as defined here.

The four counters (primitive facts)

Visit
One session by one person in one arm. The denominator of everything "per visit".
Order
One completed purchase (one checkout). A visit has 0 or 1+ orders.
Unit
One item inside an order. An order has 1+ units.
Demand
Dollars of gross order value. The only counter measured in money. (Adobe's term; elsewhere "revenue" or "GMV".)

The ratio metrics (derived, never primitive)

CVR — ConVersion Rate
orders ÷ visits. "How often does a visit buy?" A behavior metric.
AOV — Average Order Value
demand ÷ orders. "How big is an order?" A mix metric, whale-dominated at small n.
UPT — Units Per Transaction
units ÷ orders. "How many items per basket?"
AUR — Average Unit Retail
demand ÷ units. "What does one item cost?"
RPV — Revenue Per Visit
demand ÷ visits. The headline metric. Identity: RPV = CVR × AOV = CVR × UPT × AUR.
Cart-add rate
cart-adding visits ÷ visits. Needs a fifth counter (cart events) — not derivable from the four. The funnel gate upstream of orders.

Experiment terms

Arm
One of the randomized groups: control (existing experience) or test/treatment (new experience).
Lift
test ÷ control − 1, as a percentage. Read lift, never one arm's raw trend — shared shocks (promos, traffic mix) cancel in the ratio.
Percentage point (pp)
An absolute move of a rate: CVR 2.0% → 2.5% is +0.5pp (which is +25% relative). Saying a bare "%" for a rate's move is fifty-fold ambiguous — always disambiguate.
Log points
log(1+ℓ) — lift measured in log space, where undoing = negating and compounding = adding. ≈ the percentage for small lifts.
Geometric mean
The mean taken in log space: exp(mean(log xᵢ)). The honest average for ratios and lifts; the arithmetic mean of ratios is biased upward.
Pooled metric
A period ratio computed as sum(numerator) ÷ sum(denominator) — volume-weighted. Opposed to averaging daily ratios, which lets tiny days vote equally.

Noise terms

Whale
A single extreme order that dominates an average. The reason daily AOV at ~10 orders/arm is unreadable.
Heavy tail
A distribution where rare huge values carry a big share of the total (order values, latencies). Means mislead; medians and pooling resist.
Median (p50)
The middle value: half above, half below. Ignores outlier magnitude entirely — the default summary for heavy-tailed data.
Standard error (SE)
The standard deviation of an estimate: how much p̂ or x̄ would wobble across identical repeats. For a rate: SE(p̂) = √(p(1−p)/n). Shrinks with √n.
Binomial
The distribution of a count of successes in n independent yes/no trials: O ~ Binomial(V, p). Daily orders are a binomial draw even when true CVR never changes.
√-count rule
A count of rare events wobbles by about its own square root: O ± √O, so relative noise ≈ 1/√O. Ten orders is a ±32% instrument.
Log-normal
A heavy-tailed distribution where log X is bell-shaped — the standard model for money-like data. Median < mean; rare huge values carry a big share of the total.
Breakdown point
The fraction of data an adversary must corrupt to move a statistic arbitrarily. Mean: 0% (one whale suffices). Median: 50%.
Capping (winsorizing)
Clipping extreme values (e.g. order values at p99) before averaging: a small deliberate bias bought in exchange for a large variance cut. Industry standard for revenue metrics.

Process traps

Regression to the mean
After an extreme reading, the next reading from a stable process is expected at the mean — so anything done at a trough is followed by "improvement" ~Φ(2) ≈ 98% of the time. The engine of superstition loops.
Tampering
Adjusting a stable process based on individual noisy readings (Deming). Adds variance rather than removing it, and makes the treatment a moving target no experiment can measure.
Peeking
Checking results repeatedly and stopping at the first significant reading. Inflates the 5% false-alarm rate to ~26%. Legal alternatives: pre-scheduled readouts or sequential bounds.
