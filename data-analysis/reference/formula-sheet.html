Every load-bearing formula in the course — the symbols, the sentence, the intuition. Each card links back to the lesson that builds it from scratch.

The ledger (lesson 0001)

RPV ≡ CVR · AOV
Revenue Per Visit ≡ ConVersion Rate × Average Order Value
Revenue splits into how often × how big. No dollar can move RPV without moving a leg — decompose before believing any story.
lesson 0001
RPV ≡ CVR · UPT · AUR
…× Units Per Transaction × Average Unit Retail
And how big splits into items-per-basket × price-per-item. AOV stories are UPT stories or AUR stories — find out which.
lesson 0001
ℓ = T/C − 1
lift = test ÷ control − 1
Read lift, never one arm's raw trend: promo days, traffic mix, weather — anything hitting both arms cancels in the ratio.
lesson 0001
(1+ℓRPV) = (1+ℓCVR)(1+ℓAOV)
(1 + RPV lift) = (1 + CVR lift) × (1 + AOV lift)
Lifts compound multiplicatively: (1+a)(1+b) = 1 + a + b + ab. The corner term ab is what additive intuition misses — ±10% nets −1%, ±50% nets −25% (see the rectangle in the lesson).

Counting noise (lesson 0002)

SE(p̂) = √( p(1−p) / n )
Standard Error = √(one visit's variance ÷ visits)
How much an observed rate wobbles across identical days. The error bar every daily CVR silently carries.
lesson 0002
noise ∝ 1/√n
noise shrinks with the square root of sample size
Halving noise costs 4× the data. Why pooling weeks works and staring at days can't.
lesson 0002
O ± √O  →  rel. noise ≈ 1/√O
a count of rare events wobbles by its own square root
The mental shortcut: 10 orders = ±32% instrument, 250 orders = ±6%. Run it before any argument about a daily number.
lesson 0002
SEΔ = √(SEC² + SET²)
gap noise = hypotenuse of the two arms' noises
A test-vs-control gap carries both arms' wobble; independent noises add in quadrature, like perpendicular distances.
lesson 0002
z = Δ / SEΔ  ·  |z|≥2 decide, |z|≥3 breakage
z = the gap, measured in noise units
"Is this gap bigger than what identical arms produce by luck?" z<2 = breathing. The 5% guarantee belongs to ONE pre-committed look.
lesson 0002
CI = Δ ± 2·SEΔ
confidence interval = gap ± two noise units
z, the CI, and "stat sig" are one computation in three costumes: |z|≥2 ⟺ the 95% CI excludes zero ⟺ p ≤ 0.05. (Formalized in lesson 0007.)

Heavy tails (lesson 0003)

x̄ = (1/n) Σ xᵢ  →  each order votes at weight 1/n
every dollar enters the average, uncapped
On a 10-order day one $1,500 basket contributes $150 of AOV by itself. Breakdown point: mean 0%, median 50%.
lesson 0003
whale ≈ total − (n−1) × typical
back the big order out of the day's demand
The 30-second audit for any suspicious AOV day. Aug 24: 2,527 − 9×110 ≈ $1,537 — one basket was 61% of the arm's revenue.
lesson 0003
cap at p99, then average
winsorized mean: clip the top 1% of order values first
A small deliberate bias bought in exchange for a big variance cut — the industry-standard revenue metric. Median answers "typical"; capped mean answers "total, whale-resistant".
lesson 0003

Reading while running (lesson 0004)

E[Xnext | Xtoday] = μ
tomorrow's expectation ignores today's extreme
Regression to the mean: troughs recover by arithmetic. Anything shipped at a trough gets credit it didn't earn.
lesson 0004
P(recovery after a 2σ-bad day) = Φ(2) ≈ 98%
a placebo shipped on a terrible day "works" 98% of the time
The engine of the tampering loop, priced exactly. A fix must beat this number, not just beat yesterday.
lesson 0004
peeking: 5% → ≈26%
continuous checking inflates the false-alarm rate 5-fold
Stop-at-first-significance lets noise take its best of many tries. Legal versions: scheduled readouts, or sequential bounds.
lesson 0004
detection time ∝ 1/effect²
half the effect takes four times as long to prove
Catastrophes clear |z|≥3 in days; only small losses take weeks — and those are invisible to the daily dashboard too. "No time to wait" refutes itself.
lesson 0004

Lifts, ratios & logs (lesson 0005)

undo(ℓ) = 1/(1+ℓ) − 1
the same gap quoted from the other arm is the reciprocal of ℓ, not its negation
undo(−20%) = +25%; undo(−50%) = +100%. Down moves are always bigger than the same-numbered up move — flip the currency pair: test/control = 0.5 is control/test = 2.0.
lesson 0005
log(1+ℓRPV) = log(1+ℓCVR) + log(1+ℓAOV)
in log space the ledger compounds by plain addition — exactly
Logs are the coordinate system where multiplicative facts add: undoing = negating, compounding = summing, and variance decomposes.
lesson 0005
log(1+x) ≈ x  (small x)
for small lifts, log points ≈ the percentage
+3% ≈ +0.03 log points. The two languages agree for small moves and diverge for big ones — precisely when percent intuition starts lying.
lesson 0005
never average ratios — multiply them out
the arithmetic mean of lifts is biased upward; average the logs instead (geometric mean)
+100% and −50% "average" +25% but net exactly 0%. Live example: desktop daily RPV lifts average +41% while the true cumulative is +11.7%.
