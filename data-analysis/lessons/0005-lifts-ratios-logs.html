Lifts, Ratios & Logs

Percentages are a broken language for multiplicative facts. Logarithms are the coordinate system where growth math becomes honest addition.

The win By the end (~10 min): you can catch the three ways percentages mislead (asymmetry, fake addition, base confusion), undo any lift exactly, and convert a multiplicative story to log space when the math needs to be honest.

01Percentages commit three sins

Sin 1 — asymmetry. −10% is not the opposite of +10%. To undo a lift you need its reciprocal, not its negation:

undo(ℓ) = 1/(1+ℓ) − 1
the lift that exactly reverses ℓ = one over (one plus ℓ), minus one

undo(−10%) = 1/0.9 − 1 = +11.1%        undo(−50%) = 1/0.5 − 1 = +100%

Down moves are always bigger than the same-numbered up move — a stock that loses half needs to double.

Sin 2 — fake addition. Lifts compound multiplicatively (lesson 0001's rectangle: the corner term ab). +10% and −10% net −1%, and the error grows with the square of the moves.

Sin 3 — base confusion. "CVR went up 1%" — from 2.0%, is that 2.02% (+1% relative) or 3.0% (+1 percentage point = +50% relative)? Fifty-fold ambiguity in one sentence. Say pp for absolute moves of a rate, % only for relative ones.

02The fix: logs make growth additive

From the decoder, the one identity: log turns × into +. Apply it to everything above:

log(T/C) = log T − log C
a ratio becomes a difference — the "log lift", measured in log points

log(1+ℓRPV) = log(1+ℓCVR) + log(1+ℓAOV)
in log space the ledger identity compounds by PLAIN ADDITION — exactly, not approximately

log(1+x) ≈ x  (small x)
for small lifts, log points ≈ the percentage — a +3% lift is ≈ +0.03 log points

And the asymmetry dissolves: undoing a move is just negating it — log(1.1) = +0.0953 and log(1/1.1) = −0.0953, a perfect mirror. The reason ±10% "don't cancel" in percent space is that they were never equal-sized moves; log space shows their true weights (log 1.1 = +0.095 vs log 0.9 = −0.105).

04The real month, both ways

Mobile cumulative, from the Adobe sheet: CVR lift +11.8%, AOV lift −7.8%. RPV:

percent space:  (1.118) × (0.922) = 1.0308  →  +3.1%   (naive addition says +4.0%)
log space:      +0.1116 + (−0.0812) = +0.0304  →  e0.0304 = 1.0308  ✓ same answer, by addition
Trap — never average daily lift percentages The arithmetic mean of ratios is biased upward: a +100% day and a −50% day "average" to +25%, but ×2 × ×0.5 = ×1 — the true net is 0%. This is live in our data: desktop daily RPV lifts average +41%, while the honest cumulative is +11.7%. To summarize a run of lifts, multiply them out (or average the logs — the geometric mean); never average the percents.
check yourself: three quick conversions

1) A metric fell 20%. What lift restores it exactly?   2) CVR moved 2.4% → 3.0%. Express in pp and in %.   3) Daily lifts +50%, −40%, +10%. Net?

1) undo(−20%) = 1/0.8 − 1 = +25%
2) +0.6pp, which is +25% relative — say both, never a bare "up 0.6%"
3) 1.5 × 0.6 × 1.1 = 0.99 → −1%  (the naive sum says +20% — sin 2 at work)

05Check yourself

How you'd say it to a stakeholder Percentages don't add — they stack like interest. Up 30% then down 30% isn't even, it's down 9%, because the drop was measured off a bigger base. So when I combine a conversion gain with an order-size loss, I multiply the moves out rather than netting them in my head — that's why my number is sometimes a point or two off from the quick mental math, and it's the accurate one.

06Go deeper

Primary source: Spiegelhalter, The Art of Statistics, ch. 1 — on relative vs absolute risk: the pp-vs-% confusion is the single most common way honest numbers mislead the public, and his examples are devastating.

Ask your teacher (the agent) anything unclear. Good follow-ups: "why is the mean of ratios biased up but the mean of logs isn't?" (Jensen's inequality, gently), "when is naive addition of lifts acceptable?" (when moves are small enough that the corner terms are dust).
